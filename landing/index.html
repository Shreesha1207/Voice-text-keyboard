One app, two tools

They install as a single background process with one tray icon and one login. But they are two products with two prices and two independent trials — so a lapsed Dictation plan never takes your Writing access down with it, and buying one never starts the other’s clock.

Product one — Dictation

Talking is faster than typing. Now it goes in the right box.

No dictation window to open, nothing to copy across. Hold your key in a browser, in Word, in Slack, in a terminal — the transcription is inserted at the cursor you were already sitting at.

Between the key press and the text

01 Capture PyAudio reads the mic in 30 ms frames, 16 kHz mono, for exactly as long as the key is down.
02 Filter WebRTC VAD drops silence and room noise. Only speech frames are kept — this happens on your machine.
03 Normalise FFmpeg applies loudnorm, so a quiet room and a loud one reach the model at the same level.
04 Queue Audio reaches the backend and enters a Redis priority queue. Paid traffic is drained before trial traffic.
05 Transcribe A worker runs it through gpt-4o-transcribe, then optionally gpt-4o to translate.
06 Insert Text returns and is pasted at your cursor, with per-character typing as an automatic fallback.
Accents

English, the way you actually say it

Tuned for Indian, American, Australian, British, French, German and Dutch English among others. The voice filter runs in its least aggressive mode on purpose — aggressive filtering clips speech patterns with a different cadence.

Transliteration

Speak another language, get Latin script

Say something in Hindi or Kannada and Xvoice writes it phonetically in the English alphabet, rather than translating the meaning or emitting a script your reader cannot follow.

Translation PRO

Speak one language, type another

Turn Translation on from the tray menu. Your speech is transcribed, then put through a strict literal translation step into your target language before it reaches the page.

Hotkey PRO

F8 is a default, not a rule

Pick any of F2–F12, Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Space, Tab, Caps Lock, Esc, Enter, the arrows, Insert, Delete, Home, End, Page Up or Down — or any single letter or number. Change it on the dashboard; the desktop app picks it up.

Language PRO

93 transcription languages

Tell the model which language to expect, from Afrikaans to Chinese. Trial accounts are transcribed as English; Pro accounts choose.

Priority

Your audio goes to the front

Two queues, not one. The worker empties the paid queue before it looks at the trial queue, so a busy evening is felt by trials first.

Every word you dictate is counted

The dashboard is not an afterthought — for a lot of people it is the reason the app stays installed. Everything is computed in your own timezone, which the desktop app syncs on its own, so your day rolls over at your midnight rather than someone else’s.

The menu

Six actions, one click away

Improve writing, Professional tone, Make it shorter, Translate, Fix grammar and Summarize — the six that actually get used. The API behind them also serves Expand, Casual, Persuasive and Rephrase.

Formatting

It comes back looking like your document

Line breaks, punctuation, emojis, numbering and bullet lists survive the round trip. The model is told to return the transformed text and nothing else — no preamble, no summary of what it changed.

Languages

60 translation targets

English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Hindi sit in the menu out of the box. Another 54 — Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Arabic, Korean, Polish, Swahili, Welsh and more — are added from the settings page, up to 12 pinned at a time.

Consent

Your clipboard is untouched until you click

Right-clicking offers the button; nothing is read from your screen until you actually click it. A stray selection, or a right-click on its own, never copies anything.

Control

Preview first, or replace immediately

Read the rewrite and accept it, or have it go straight in. Accepting restores focus to the editor you were in before it pastes, so the text lands in the right place.

Memory

Only a snippet is kept

The history view renders a 120-character excerpt, so only 200 characters of each input and output are stored. What you highlight is not archived in full, forever.

Independent

One product lapsing never touches the other

Each product carries its own status, its own renewal date and its own subscription id. A cancelled Dictation plan expires on its own schedule and leaves Writing exactly where it was.

Separate trials

Buying one never burns the other’s trial

Dictation and Writing each get a full 14 days. Starting or paying for one does not consume, shorten or start the other.

Cancelling

You keep what you paid for

Cancel and access continues to the end of the period you already paid for, then stops. Billing is handled in the Stripe customer portal, from your dashboard.

macOS note

There is no Dock icon — look up, not down

Xvoice is a menu-bar app by design. It is the microphone at the top right of your screen, next to the clock — the same role the system tray plays on Windows. If you installed it and “nothing happened”, it is almost certainly up there.

macOS permissions

Three switches, opened for you

Microphone to hear you, Accessibility to type into other apps, and Input Monitoring to notice your hotkey while another app is in front. The first-run helper opens all three panes.

If it misbehaves

A diagnostic that tells you the fix

python3 mac/preflight.py checks architecture match, quarantine, signature, bundle plist, every dependency and each permission — then prints the exact command that fixes what it found.

Security & privacy

An app that listens to you should be boring about your data.

No API keys

You never handle an OpenAI key

The key is held server-side and the desktop app never sees it. You sign in through your browser once; the app stays linked until you log out.

Sessions

Log out everywhere, instantly

One click invalidates every access and refresh token on every device at once — desktop and browser — by bumping a version the server checks on every single request.

Logs

Nothing you say is written to disk

The log file records lengths and status codes, never content. Not what you dictated, not what you rewrote.

Prompt injection

Your text is data, never instructions

The translation step and every writing action tell the model to treat the input strictly as text to transform — never to execute, answer or obey anything inside it. A pasted email cannot hijack the rewrite.

Credentials

Hashed, rotated, time-limited

Passwords are bcrypt-hashed. Reset links expire in 15 minutes and invalidate every active session when used. Google sign-in is there if you would rather have no password at all.

Billing

Signed sync, verified webhooks

Subscription updates are HMAC-SHA256 signed, and Stripe webhook signatures are verified before anything is written — so nobody can spoof a paid status into your account.

Under the hood

Built to keep running when nobody is watching.

A background utility fails differently from an app with a window: nobody sees it die. So the parts that can crash are supervised, and the parts that need the network wait for it.

Watchdogs

It restarts itself

The keyboard listener and the recording loop each run under a supervisor. If either dies — a screen lock, a driver hiccup — it is back within seconds.

Single instance

One app, however often you launch it

A loopback socket holds the lock. A second launch pings the running copy, which raises a notification and opens your dashboard instead of starting a duplicate.

Offline

It waits instead of failing

No connection at startup means it polls three public resolvers and picks up the moment the network returns, rather than dying at launch.

Sessions

Silent token refresh

An expired access token is renewed in the background from the stored refresh token. You only see a browser prompt if that fails too.

Questions

Before you download

Do I need my own OpenAI API key?

No. Xvoice is a hosted service — the key lives on the Xvoice backend and the desktop app never sees it. You sign in through your browser, and that is the whole setup.

Does it work in every application?

Dictation works anywhere a text cursor does: browsers, Word, Notepad, Slack, email clients, editors, terminals. Writing works on any text you can select and copy.

What happens when my trial ends?

Each product has its own 14-day trial and its own ending. When the Dictation trial ends the hotkey stops transcribing and the app tells you why. Writing falls back to the free monthly allowance. Nothing is deleted — your stats, streaks and achievements are waiting if you come back.

If I buy Dictation, do I get Writing too?

Not in full. Dictation Pro includes 100 writing actions a month — enough to get a real feel for it — but unlimited Writing means Writing Pro or the Platform bundle. The two are priced separately on purpose.

Is my dictation stored anywhere?

The audio file is deleted on the server as soon as it has been transcribed. Logs record lengths and status codes, never content. For Writing, only a 200-character excerpt of each input and output is kept, to render the history list.

Can I change the hotkey?

Yes, on Pro. F2–F12, Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Space, Tab, Caps Lock, Esc, Enter, the arrow keys, Insert, Delete, Home, End, Page Up or Down, or any single letter or number. Set it on the dashboard and the desktop app picks it up. Writing has its own separate hotkey.

Which accents does it handle?

English spoken in Indian, American, Australian, British, French, German and Dutch accents, among others. It is a design goal rather than a side effect — the noise filter is deliberately tuned to avoid clipping speech patterns with a different cadence.

Why does macOS say the app is damaged?

The build is not notarized yet, so macOS quarantines it on download. The included first-run helper clears that, or you can run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Xvoice.app.

Can I run it from source?

Yes — the desktop client is MIT-licensed and on GitHub, with setup scripts for Windows, macOS and Linux and build scripts for every installer. The backend is a FastAPI service in the same repository.

How do I cancel?

Through the Stripe customer portal, linked from your billing dashboard. You keep access until the end of the period you have already paid for.
